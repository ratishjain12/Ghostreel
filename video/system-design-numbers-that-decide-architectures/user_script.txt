Most system design decisions come down to a handful of numbers. Know these, and half the architecture draws itself.

First, reading from memory takes about a hundred nanoseconds. Reading from an SSD takes around a hundred microseconds. That thousand x gap is the whole reason caches exist.

Second, a round trip inside one data center is about half a millisecond. Across continents, it's over a hundred. That's why you replicate data close to your users instead of calling home.

Third, a single well tuned Postgres box handles tens of thousands of simple queries a second. Most apps never outgrow it, so shard last, not first.

Fourth, Postgres connections are expensive. A few hundred is a practical ceiling, which is why every serious setup puts a connection pooler in front.

Fifth, one server can hold tens of thousands of idle WebSocket connections. Memory per connection, not CPU, is what decides how many machines you need.

When you're unsure about a design, do the math with these first. The numbers usually make the decision for you.
