Most AI agents are just a for loop with a prompt. Here are the eight patterns that actually make them work.

First. Tool use. The model doesn't call functions directly. It emits a structured call, your code runs it, and the result goes back in as a new message.

Second. ReAct. Reason, then act, then observe, and repeat. You can't know what to search next until you see what the last search returned.

Third. Plan and execute. One model plans every step upfront, then just runs the plan. More predictable, less adaptive than ReAct.

Fourth. Agentic RAG. The model decides if it even needs to retrieve, what to search, and whether the results are good enough before it answers.

Fifth. Sequential. Agents run in a fixed pipeline. Draft, review, format. Each one's output feeding the next.

Sixth. Parallel. Independent agents run at the same time on different pieces of the task, then their outputs get merged.

Seventh. Router. One agent classifies the task and sends it to whichever specialist actually handles it.

Eighth. Orchestrator worker. A manager agent breaks the task down, assigns it to workers, checks their results, and decides what happens next.
