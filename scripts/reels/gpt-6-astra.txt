OpenAI just shipped a model that doesn't want to just answer you — it wants to operate your computer. Meet GPT-6 Astra, released September third, 2026.

Astra's actual pitch is different: it browses, it codes, it uses apps, it builds websites, it runs its own QA, and, this stopped people mid-scroll, it can model a house in Blender and turn it into a walkable Unreal Engine 5 scene.

On OSWorld 2.0, a real computer-use benchmark, Astra scored 72.6%, in about 40 minutes per task. On BenchCAD, its 3D and CAD reasoning benchmark, it hit 95.9%.

Then there's the part that isn't a flex. Astra is OpenAI's first model to cross the "Critical" cybersecurity threshold in their own safety framework, meaning it can find previously unknown vulnerabilities and build exploits for them, without a person guiding each step. It scored 100% on ExploitBench.

OpenAI also says an internal version contributed to progress on ten long-standing problems in math and computer science, though one of those results is explicitly conditional on unproven assumptions, not an unconditional proof, so that claim needs the asterisk.

Astra doesn't win everything, Claude Fable 5.1 still beats it on Humanity's Last Exam. This isn't AGI. But it's a model built to operate, not just to answer, and that's a real shift.
